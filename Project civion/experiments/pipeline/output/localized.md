## [S023] PART 2 - PRODUCTS > 2.5 CEMENT MATERIALS

## [S024] PART 2 - PRODUCTS > 2.6 WATER

- A. ## [S027] PART 2 - PRODUCTS > 2.8 AGGREGATES FOR CONCRETE

- A. Coarse Aggregate

1. Coarse aggregate shall be natural materials such as stone, gravel etc-crushed, uncrushed or a combination thereof-obtained from approved quarries. Aggregate shall be hard, strong, dense, durable, clean and free from veins and adherent coating. It shall be free from soft, friable, flat, thin, elongated or laminated pieces and shall be roughly cubical in shape. It shall consist of coarse material most of which is retained on 4.75 mm IS sieve.
2. Coarse aggregate shall not contain any harmful material such as iron, pyrites, coal, mica shale or similar laminated material or clay, alkali, soft fragments, sea shells, organic impurities etc. in quantities that can adversely affect the strength and durability of the concrete. In addition to the above, in reinforced concrete the aggregate shall not contain any material that might attack the reinforcement. The maximum quantities of deleterious materials in the coarse aggregate when determined in accordance with IS 2386 Part I and Part II "Method of test for aggregates for concrete' shall not exceed the limits provided in Table -- 8.
3. Aggregate crushing value, impact value, abrasion value and soundness of aggregate shall respectively be in accordance with paragraphs 3.3, 3.4, 3.5 and 3.6 of IS 383.
4. Grading of coarse aggregate shall conform to the requirements in IS 383. See Table - 9.
5. Source of aggregate shall be an approved Government location. It shall be tested prior to the approval of the ER by an approved testing laboratory. In case available aggregates do not meet specific requirements of IS 383 or any other applicable specification, required processing shall be carried out by the Contractor at his cost. It shall be the duty of the Contractor to make sure that aggregate material received by him is from Government approve quarries and with fully paid royalties, taxes, duties etc. as may be in force from time to time for respective locations.
6. Aggregate shall be stored in such a way that it does not get mixed with mud, grass vegetables and other foreign matter. The best way ensure this is to have a hard surface platform made out of concrete, bricks or planks. It should meet the approval of the ER.
7. Coarse aggregate shall have a minimum specific gravity of 2.6 (Saturated surface dry basis). Aggregate below this specific gravity shall not be used without the special permission of the ER.
8. Once a specific source of supply of coarse aggregate is approved, the source shall not be changed without the prior approval of the ER.
- B. Fine Aggregate
1. Natural sand deposited by stream or glacial agencies as a result of disintegration of rock is the best form of fine aggregate. The fine aggregate shall confirm to following standards.
- a. For Plain And Reinforced Concrete: IS 383 Specification for coarse and fine aggregates from natural sources for concrete.

2. Can be obtained from crushed stone screening but often contains a high percentage of dust and clay. It tends to be flaky and angular. This type produces harsh concrete and should be avoided.
3. Sea sand shall not be used unless approved by the ER. If approved, the required treatment shall be done at the Contractor's cost.
4. Sand shall be hard, durable, clean and free from adherent coatings and organic matter and shall not contain any appreciable amount of clay. Sand shall not contain harmful impurities such as iron, pyrites, coal particles, lignite, mica shale or similar laminated material, alkalis, and organic impurities in such form or quantity as to affect the strength or durability of concrete. It should also not contain any material liable to attack the steel reinforcement.
5. When tested per IS 2386 Part I and Part II, deleterious materials in fine aggregate shall not exceed permissible limits given in Table -- 8.
6. Fine aggregate shall be thoroughly washed at site with clean fresh water such that the percentage of all deleterious materials is within the permissible limits laid down.
7. Grading of fine aggregate shall conform to IS 383 and shall fall within limits of one of the four zones given in table 4 of IS 383. See Table -- 10.
8. Damp and moist sand increases in volume and this effect is called bulking. Due allowance shall be made for this effect when preparing mixes based on volume measurements. Bulking effects shall be determined per IS 2386 Part III Appendix A. For rough guidance Table --12 gives the relation between moisture content and percentage of bulking.
9. Storing of aggregate shall be as given in clause 2.8.6.

Also it is important that the mix should be easily worked with the help of equipment available at site. ## [S052] PART 3 - EXECUTION > 3.6 CONCRETE > G. Compaction of Concrete

1. Concrete shall be thoroughly compacted, as depositing shall proceed by means of suitable vibrators. The vibrators shall maintain the entire concrete under treatment in an adequate state of agitation that shall continue during the whole period of placing of concrete. Care shall be taken not to over- vibrate the concrete. While withdrawing needles no holes shall be visible in concrete. Compacting shall be completed before the initial setting time. Concrete already set shall not be disturbed by successive vibrations.
2. Insert and withdraw vibrators vertically drawing out entrapped air and excess water. Do not use vibrators to transport concrete within forms. Do not insert vibrators into lower layers of concrete that have begun to set. At each insertion limit duration of vibration to time necessary to consolidate concrete and complete embedment of reinforcement and other embedded items without causing segregation of mix. Pay particular attention to providing adequate vibration in the vicinity of sleeves and embeds in heavily reinforced elements and other elements as required. Provide form vibrators to ensure maximum density of concrete.
3. It shall be ensured that the needle vibrators are not applied to reinforcement, which may destroy the bond between concrete and reinforcement. When electric vibrators are in use, a standby petrol vibrator shall always be available at the concreting point.

- A. Independent Testing Agency: A testing agency, engaged at the Contractor's expense, will perform the activities listed herein.
- B. Monitor the Contractor's Quality Control Program. The monitoring activities do not relieve the Contractor of sole responsibility for maintaining the Quality Control Program.
- C. Monitoring of Concrete Placement: Continuously observe concrete placement operations on a full-time basis, record such observations on a daily basis, and submit reports of the results.
- D. Monitoring of Contractor's Testing: Monitor Contractor's non-destructive strength testing for form removal and termination of curing.
- E. Compression Tests: Sample at point of deposit. Cure the cylinders in laboratory.
1. Additional Cylinders: Furnish a supply of 25 standard cylinder molds at the site at all times for making additional cylinders as may be directed by the ER.
2. Quantity of test cubes and testing
- a. The minimum frequency of cube casting for testing shall be as follows. Each sample shall consist of 6 cubes.

| Concrete quantity           | Number of Samples.                           |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Up to 5 cu.m. in a day      | 1                                            |
| 5 cu.m. to 15 cu.m.         | 2                                            |
| 15 cu.m. to 30 cu.m.        | 3                                            |
| 30 cu.m. to 50 cu.m.        | 4                                            |
| More than 50 cu.m. thereof. | 4 + one additional per each 50 cu.m. or part |

- b. Three cubes shall be tested on the 7 th day and other three cubes on the 28 th day.
- c. For Mass Concrete, One additional sample shall be taken and reserved for testing at 56 days.
- d. Acceptance of Work

This shall be as given in IS 456, SP 23 and SP 24. Part or element of Work shall be deemed to be accepted, provided the results of the 28 th day cube testing conform to the criteria stated as under

- 1) The average of the three consecutive cube's strength shall not be less than the specified strength.
- 2) No individual cube strength shall be less than 90% of the specified strength.
- 3) If an individual cube strength is more than 33% below the specified strength, such a cube shall be classified as a freak and the criteria in (a) and (b) shall be applied to the remaining two cubes and their acceptability determined.
- 4) If the concrete tests fail to meet the acceptance criteria of the minimum strength required for respective grades of concrete, the ER may take one of the following decisions:
- 5) Instruct the Contractor to carry out such additional tests (e.g. core tests, load tests etc.) and / or remedial measures to ensure the soundness of the structure at the Contractor's expense.
- a) Any decision to accept the Work shall be entirely at the discretion of the ER who may order a reduction in the rate of the appropriate item.
- b) The Work will be rejected and any consequential action as needed shall be taken at the Contractor's expense including cutting out and replacing a part or whole of the Work.
- e. Compression Test Reports: Present at least the following data in tabular form and distribute immediately after recording the test results.
- 1) Identity of job, Contractor, supplier.
- 2) Identity of mix and required strength.
- 3) Pour location of sampled concrete.
- 4) Slump, water content, air content, truck number, time and date sampled, air temperature, concrete temperature, consistency.

- 5) Curing history.
- 6) Date tested.
- 7) Compressive strength.
- 8) Type of fracture.
- 9) Compliance with specifications (yes or no)
- F. Slump Tests: First truck each day, each sample for cylinders, and as often as necessary thereafter. Provide a slump cone and rod available for use at all times. Verify water content in accordance with AASHTO T318, "Measurement of Water Content of Fresh Concrete Using the Microwave Oven" as required by the ER, but not less than one test per day.
- G. Reinforcement Material Verification: Verify materials conform with the Contract Documents.
- H. Reinforcing Placement Inspection: Periodically inspect the placement of steel reinforcing, embedments, and insert placement. Provide daily reports of compliance.
- I. Formwork Inspection: Periodically observe formwork shape, location and dimensions and shoring operations; record such observations on a daily basis.
